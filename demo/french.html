Le présentoir · à la une

This morning's headlines

Nine French sources, pulled every morning: three national dailies, two public broadcasters' France desks, two radio news feeds, the 10-minute Journal en français facile with its transcript, and 1jour1actu in plain French. Read a headline, open the story if you want, then press J'ai lu and write two sentences in French about it. Your sentences save to the department and count as distance along the boulevard. Each headline is tagged to the themes it touches; the tags are a sort, not a verdict.

Le carnet de coupures

Keep a cutting

A coupure de presse is a cutting: the piece of the paper you keep. For anything you watch, hear or read, keep one: what it was, the one French sentence that stopped you (copied exactly), what you make of it, and the question it leaves. Write the middle two in French if you can. A book of cuttings like this is where your Paper 3 independent research project, a prize essay and a personal statement come from.

What the department would see

A separate teacher page, with no setup beyond pasting a token once: every pupil who has signed in; which headlines each has read and the two French sentences they wrote on each, in full (so you see French being written every week without setting it); who tapped I want to enter for each prize (your entry list for the Anthea Bell Prize, the Joutes oratoires and the Olympiad, which go through school); how many of the half-term three each pupil has done; how far along the boulevard each is and which racks they have not touched; each pupil's carnet de coupures as copyable text for references and personal statements; who on your form list has not signed in yet; a CSV. The headline rack updates itself every morning; the rest of the content is updated each half term by editing one block at the top of the file, and pupils keep everything they have done.

Anything on this page can be changed for French: the sources on the rack, the theme keywords, the items, the half-term three, the dates, Zazie's lines, the carnet fields, what gets tracked, the look of it.
