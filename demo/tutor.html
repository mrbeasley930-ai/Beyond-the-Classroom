Beyond the Classroom · for tutors · demo

Everything your tutees do, in one place, ready for UCAS.

Four departments (Chemistry, Physics, Biology, Art) now run tracked "Beyond the Classroom" pages. Pupils sign in with their form and name, so what a pupil does in every subject can be put together under their name for their tutor. This is a working demo of that tutor view, with an invented form group.

  1. Look at the form. Who is doing what, who needs a word.
  2. Open a folder. One tutee, every subject, in their own words.
  3. Build the UCAS pack. Their evidence sorted under the three personal statement questions, plus lines for the reference.

Worth a word this week

Three plain rules, nothing clever: nothing logged for three weeks; signed in but nothing done; doing things but writing nothing down. The rules are ours to change.

What you have to do

Open this page. Read it. That is the list.

  • Nothing to set up and no spreadsheet to keep. The page pulls the four departments' records every time it opens.
  • Pupils add nothing for your benefit. They tick and write on the subject pages they already use; this view reads the same records.
  • The only rule for pupils: the same form code and full name on every subject page. The pages already ask for both.

Built in school, changed in school

The pages, the records and this view are ours. There is no supplier, no licence and no waiting list. If the tutor team wants a column, a different sort, a different set of rules for "worth a word", or an export shaped like a form you already use, that is a conversation with Simon and a change to one file.

Already possible

  • Pull one tutee's record as plain text to paste into a reference or a Unifrog note
  • CSV of the whole form for a spreadsheet
  • Head of Year view across every form
  • A half-termly summary emailed to each tutor
  • Pupils' own view of the same record (see What a pupil sees)

Easy to add

  • Your existing UCAS tracking columns (target course, predicted grades entered by hand, statement draft status)
  • Work experience and volunteering logged by the pupil, so question 3 fills too
  • A tutor note on each folder that only tutors see
  • Deadlines for the competitions a pupil has put their hand up for
  • Flags set by Heads of Year rather than by Simon

As more departments join

  • Each new subject page appears here on its own, under the same names
  • History, Economics, Maths and the rest are already written as resource lists; the tracked pages follow department by department
  • Nothing changes for tutors when a subject is added

UCAS pack · personal statement evidence

Sorted under the three questions

UCAS personal statements are now three answers. This is a first sort of what the pupil has already done and written, using plain rules (talks and reading under 1, practice and competitions under 2, visits, events and programmes under 3). Move anything with the 1 2 3 buttons; × drops it. The pupil writes the statement; this is the raw material, with dates, in their own words.

For the reference · "supportive information"

Drafted from the record. Facts and dates only, in the order they happened. Edit, then copy.

Everything, in date order

In their own words

Every notebook entry the pupil wrote on a subject page: what they did, what they took from it, the question it left them with. This is what a personal statement is made of.

Competitions they have put their hand up for

What a pupil sees

The same record from the pupil's side. They sign in once with their form and name and see every subject's page they have used, and their evidence already sorted for UCAS. They type nothing new here. The point for the pupil: from the first week of Year 12, every talk, visit and competition is banked with a date and their own sentence about it, so the statement in Year 13 is assembled rather than remembered.
