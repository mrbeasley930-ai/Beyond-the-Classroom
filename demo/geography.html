Geography · The Overpass · demo

A demo of a web page for your sixth-form geographers.

It is a working copy of the page a pupil would use: 56 free things to watch, listen to, read, map, enter and apply for, each tagged to a topic of the Edexcel course. It is laid out as the mission display at a satellite ground station. The satellite flies six passes over the Earth, one for each kind of thing; every item is a frame along a pass, and each thing a pupil does images one frame and sharpens the satellite's picture. The hazard board collects real earthquakes, storms, eruptions, floods and geography news every morning, plots them on the map, and pupils write two sentences on one that the department can read.

7 · The hazard board · refreshed every morning

The Earth this week

Real hazards and geography news, collected automatically every morning. The hazards come from the US Geological Survey (earthquakes of magnitude 5 and over), NASA's Earth Observatory Natural Event Tracker (EONET), and GDACS, the disaster alert service run by the UN and the European Commission; they are also plotted on the map above. The stories come from nine sources. Pick one, open it, then press Log it and write two sentences: what happened, and the geography behind it. It saves to the department and goes into your scene logs. Each item is tagged to the topics of the course it seems to touch. The tags come from keywords, so some will be wrong; finding a better link to the course is part of the exercise.

Scene logs

Log every scene

Every image that comes down from a satellite has a log: where, when, what it shows. Do the same for anything you watch, hear, read, map or enter: what it was, the geographical idea in one line, what you found out, and a question it left you with or where you would look next. These logs are where a personal statement, an interview answer and an NEA question come from.

What the department would see

A separate teacher page, with no setup beyond pasting a token once: every pupil who has signed in; who tapped I want to enter for each competition (your list for the Olympiad and the RGS essay, and a heads-up on everyone entering prizes and applying to summer schools on their own); how much of the half-term acquisition plan each pupil has done; each pupil's coverage, resolution and which of the six passes they have used; every hazard and story each pupil has logged from the hazard board, with the two sentences they wrote; each pupil's scene logs in full, as copyable text for references and personal statements; which topics of the course the class is reading around and which it is not; who on your form list has not signed in yet; a CSV. The acquisition plan is edited in one block at the top of the file; the hazard board updates itself; pupils keep everything they have done.
