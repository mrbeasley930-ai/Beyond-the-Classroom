Computer Science · The Repo · demo

A demo of a web page for your sixth-form computer scientists.

It is a working copy of the page a pupil would use: 58 free things to listen to, watch, read, build, enter and apply for, each tagged to a part of OCR H446. It is laid out as the pupil's own code repository. Six folders, one per kind of thing; every item is an open issue, and doing it closes the issue. Each folder is a branch that merges into main when every issue in it is closed, and everything a pupil does lands on a contributions graph for the school year. Upstream collects computer science news and the latest Computerphile videos every morning, and pupils write two sentences on one that the department can read.

Computer science in the news

Collected automatically every morning from the BBC technology desk, the National Cyber Security Centre, The Conversation, Quanta Magazine, the Raspberry Pi Foundation, Ars Technica, IEEE Spectrum, MIT News and ScienceDaily, plus the latest Computerphile videos. Read or watch one, then press Pull it and write two sentences: what happened, and the computer science behind it (the hardware, the algorithm, the protocol, the data, the law). It saves to the department and goes into your commit log. Each item is tagged to the parts of H446 it seems to touch. The tags come from keywords, so some will be wrong; finding a better link to the course is part of the exercise.

Write a commit

A good commit message says what changed in one line, and the body says why. Write one for anything you watch, read, build or enter: what it was, the idea in one line, what you found out, and what you would try next. These commits are where a personal statement, an interview answer and a reference come from.

What the department would see

A separate teacher page, with no setup beyond pasting a token once: every pupil who has signed in; who tapped I want to enter or I'm interested for each competition and placement (your lists for Bebras, BIO, the Coding Challenge, PCTC and Astro Pi, and a heads-up on who is entering the Cipher Challenge or applying for UNIQ, Nuffield or CyberFirst on their own); how much of the half-term milestone each pupil has done; each pupil's issues closed, branches merged and contributions across the year, so you can see who works steadily and who does it all in one night; every news story each pupil has pulled from upstream, with the two sentences they wrote; each pupil's commit log in full, as copyable text for references and personal statements; who on your form list has not signed in yet; a CSV. The milestone is edited in one block at the top of the file; upstream updates itself; pupils keep everything they have done.
