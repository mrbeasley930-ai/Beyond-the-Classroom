English Language and Literature · The Scriptorium · demo

A demo of a web page for your Language and Literature sixth form.

It is a working copy of the page a pupil would use: 61 free things to enter, watch, listen to, read, do and apply for, each tagged to an H474 component. It is laid out as James Murray's Scriptorium, the shed where the Oxford English Dictionary was built from quotation slips sent in by volunteer readers. Each thing a pupil does files a slip in its pigeonhole. Each slip they write (headword, source, the line itself, why it matters) goes in their slip box, and the department can read it. Every historical claim on the page carries its source and a label.

The slip box

Write a slip

The dictionary's readers posted in one slip per quotation: the word, the source and the line itself, so that the editors could see the word at work. Do the same for anything you watch, hear or read. Take the line that made you stop, say where it came from and why it matters. A box of slips like this is where a personal statement, an essay prize entry and your independent study come from.

What the department would see

A separate teacher page, with no setup beyond pasting a token once: every pupil who has signed in; who tapped I want to enter for each prize (your entry list for the Linguistics Olympiad and the Campion Prize, which go through school); how many of the half-term wanted list each pupil has done; how much of the wall each has filed and which bays they have not touched; each pupil's slip box in full, as copyable text for references and personal statements; who on your form list has not signed in yet; a CSV. Content is updated each half term by editing one block at the top of the file; pupils keep everything they have done.
