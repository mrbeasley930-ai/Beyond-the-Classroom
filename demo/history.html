History · The History Line · demo

A demo of a web page for your sixth-form historians.

It is a working copy of the page a pupil would use: 80 free things to listen to, watch, read, do, enter and apply for, most of them tied to your course: 1H Russia 1855–1964, 2S Britain 1951–2007 and the seven NEA questions. It is laid out as a railway station. Six platforms, one per kind of thing; every item is a service with a destination, a place and a year (Petrograd 1917, Westminster 1967, Smethwick 1965). Doing it punches the pupil's ticket and visits a stop on that platform's line; a line runs through to its terminus when every stop is visited. The Arrivals board collects history articles and new podcast episodes every morning, and pupils write two sentences on one that the department can read.

Arrivals · new every morning

History in the news

Collected automatically every morning from the National Archives' blogs, HistoryExtra, The Conversation and the Guardian's history books desk, plus new episodes of history podcasts, most of them on the department's own list: The Rest is History, Cold War Conversations, Not Just the Tudors, Gone Medieval and the HistoryExtra podcast. Read or listen to one, then press Log it and write two sentences: what it was about, and how it connects to something you study (a cause, a consequence, a historian's view, a source you could use). It saves to the department and goes into your travel log. Each item is tagged to the part of the course it seems to touch. The tags come from keywords, so some will be wrong; finding a better link is part of the exercise.

travel log

Travel log

Write a line for anything you listen to, watch, read, visit or enter: what it was, its point in one line, what you found out, and a question it left you with. Historians are asked what they have read beyond the course and what they made of it. This log is where your answer to that comes from, and where your personal statement and your teacher's reference start.

What the department would see

A separate teacher page, with no setup beyond pasting a token once: every pupil who has signed in; who tapped I want to enter, Put my name down or I'm interested (your list for the Great Debate, Lessons from Auschwitz and the Oxford workshops, and a heads-up on who is entering the Robson, Julia Wood and John Locke prizes or applying for UNIQ on their own); how much of the half-term timetable each pupil has done; each pupil's journeys by platform and by unit (1H, 2S, NEA), so you can see who only ever does the Britain unit; every article and podcast each pupil has logged from the Arrivals board, with the two sentences they wrote; each pupil's travel log in full, as copyable text for references and personal statements; who on your form list has not signed in yet; a CSV. The half-term timetable is edited in one block at the top of the file; the Arrivals board updates itself; pupils keep everything they have done.

Anything on this page can be changed for History: the items, the unit tags, which sources the Arrivals board reads, the dates, the announcer's lines, what gets tracked, the look of it. Use the Feedback button to say what you would want.
