Engineering · The Garage · demo

A demo of a web page for your sixth-form engineers.

It is a working copy of the page a pupil would use: 50 free things to watch, listen to, read, build, enter and apply for, each tagged to a unit of the course. It is laid out as a race team's garage over one weekend. Each session is one kind of thing, with a strip of rev lights, one per item; each thing a pupil does lights one and takes time off their lap on the timing screen. The pit wall screens collect engineering news every morning, with motorsport technology alongside civil, electrical, materials and space engineering, and pupils write two sentences on a story that the department can read.

BRINDLEY RACING · CAR 27

7 · The pit wall · new stories every morning

Engineering in the news

This week's engineering stories from twelve sources, collected automatically every morning: motorsport technology from Racecar Engineering, The Race and Motorsport.com, and wider engineering from New Civil Engineer, IEEE Spectrum, the BBC, ESA, NASA, Carbon Brief, Tech Xplore and ScienceDaily. Read one, open the full story, then press Log it and write two sentences: what was built, tested or found, and the engineering principle behind it. It saves to the department and goes onto a debrief sheet. Each story is tagged to the units of the course it seems to touch. The tags come from keywords, so some will be wrong; finding a better link to the course is part of the exercise.

Debrief sheets

Debrief it while it's fresh

After every session the engineers write down what happened and why. Do the same for anything you watch, hear, read, build or enter: what it was, the engineering principle in one line, what you found out, and what you would test or change next. These sheets are where a personal statement, an interview answer and an apprenticeship application come from.

Your debrief sheets

What the department would see

A separate teacher page, with no setup beyond pasting a token once: every pupil who has signed in; who tapped I want to enter for each competition (your list for a STEM Racing or Greenpower team, and a heads-up on everyone applying for scholarships and placements on their own); how much of the half-term run plan each pupil has done; each pupil's lap time, grid slot and which of the six sessions they have used; every news story each pupil has logged from the pit wall, with the two sentences they wrote; each pupil's debrief sheets in full, as copyable text for references and personal statements; who on your form list has not signed in yet; a CSV. The run plan is edited in one block at the top of the file; the news updates itself; pupils keep everything they have done.
