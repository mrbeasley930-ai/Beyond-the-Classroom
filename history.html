Sixth form · History · 2026-27

History beyond the classroom29 things to read, hear, watch and enter. All free.

History admissions tutors ask what you have read beyond the course and what you made of it. The reading and podcasts below are chosen so you can answer; the essay prizes are set by the universities themselves. Read a primary source for any topic you study and note where it disagrees with the textbook. Each time you do something, write one line in your source book: what you did, one thing you learned, one question it left you with. That is your personal statement material.

Competitions · 4 things

Enter

Tg

The Great Debate 2027 (Historical Association)

23entryYears 10–13enter yourself

A five-minute speech answering: "How does your local environment help us to understand national and global histories?" Heats are regional; finalists get cash prizes and free HA membership.

When: Regional heats through the autumn and spring; national final in the spring. Dates by region on the HA site.

HA page  history.org.uk/secondary/categories/514/news/3886/the-great-debate-2027

Rh

Robson History Prize (Trinity College, Cambridge)

24entryY12 when questions are releasedenter yourself

Up to 3,000 words on one of a set of questions. £600 first prize, split between you and the school. No generative AI.

When: Questions released March 2027; deadline end of August 2027 (2026 deadline was 31 August, 12 noon).

Trinity  trin.cam.ac.uk/undergraduate/essay-prizes/history

Vh

Vellacott History Prize (Peterhouse, Cambridge)

25essayY12enter yourself

A history essay on a set question. Winners and commended entrants are invited to a lunch at Peterhouse with academics.

When: Questions released in the spring; deadline usually April. Confirm on Peterhouse site.

Peterhouse  pet.cam.ac.uk/essay-prizes-faqs

St Hugh's  st-hughs.ox.ac.uk/prospective-students/outreach-at-st-hughs/essay-competitions/julia-wood-history-essay-competition
