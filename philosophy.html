Sixth form · Philosophy · 2026-27

Philosophy beyond the classroom36 things to read, hear, watch and enter. Free unless the entry says otherwise.

Philosophy is argued, so the most useful thing you can do is read an argument, state it in your own words and say where it fails. The podcasts and reading below are chosen for that. The essay prizes are the test: a question, a word limit and a deadline. Each time you do something, write one line in your commonplace book: what you did, one thing you learned, one question it left you with. That is your personal statement material.

Talks and lectures · 15 things

Watch

Ch

David Chalmers: How do you explain consciousness?

1318mtalk

A 18 min talk.

Think about: Set out why Chalmers thinks consciousness is a "hard problem" for physicalism. Which physicalist theory on the AQA Metaphysics of Mind list does his argument damage most?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/david_chalmers_how_do_you_explain_consciousness

St

Anil Seth: Your brain hallucinates your conscious reality

1417mtalk

A 17 min talk.

Think about: Seth says what we perceive is the brain's best guess. Is this closer to direct realism or indirect realism (Epistemology: perception)? Give one reason.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/anil_seth_your_brain_hallucinates_your_conscious_reality

Nl

Eleanor Nelsen: Mary's Room: A philosophical thought experiment

155mTED-Ed

A 5 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: Write Jackson's knowledge argument as numbered premises, then give the ability knowledge response.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/eleanor_nelsen_mary_s_room_a_philosophical_thought_experiment

Ns

Eleanor Nelsen: Would you sacrifice one person to save five?

165mTED-Ed

A 5 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: How would an act utilitarian and a Kantian answer the switch case and the footbridge case? Which theory explains the difference between them better?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/eleanor_nelsen_would_you_sacrifice_one_person_to_save_five

Sn

Peter Singer: The why and how of effective altruism

1717mtalk

A 17 min talk.

Think about: Singer argues from utilitarianism to a duty to give. Which objection to utilitarianism on the AQA list does most damage to his case? This talk is there to be argued with.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/peter_singer_the_why_and_how_of_effective_altruism

Sd

Michael Sandel: The lost art of democratic debate

1819mtalk

A 19 min talk.

Think about: Sandel uses Aristotle's idea that things have a purpose. Explain how this links to Aristotle's function argument in virtue ethics.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/michael_sandel_the_lost_art_of_democratic_debate

Gl

Julia Galef: Why you think you're right -- even if you're wrong

1911mtalk

A 11 min talk.

Think about: Is Galef describing a condition for knowledge or a good habit for forming beliefs? Link your answer to the tripartite definition of knowledge.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/julia_galef_why_you_think_you_re_right_even_if_you_re_wrong

Kwame Anthony Appiah: Is religion good or bad? (This is a trick question)

2014mtalk

A 14 min talk.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/kwame_anthony_appiah_is_religion_good_or_bad_this_is_a_trick_question

Competitions · 5 things

Enter

Te

Think Essay Prize (Royal Institute of Philosophy)

28paperAged 15–18 at the closing date; any schoolenter yourself

Up to 1,200 words on one of six questions. The 2026 set included whether God can be known, moral responsibility in a determined universe and machine consciousness. The winner is published in Think. Any use of AI disqualifies the entry.

When: 2026 round closed 20 April 2026 and was open by mid-November 2025. Expect the 2027 round to open in the autumn with an April deadline; confirm on the RIP site.

RIP page  royalinstitutephilosophy.org/news/think-essay-prize

Pe

Practical Ethics and Responsibility Competition (Uehiro Oxford Institute)

29paperYears 11–13, UK teams onlyenter yourself

Make a short video about an ethical problem and how responsibility comes into it. Past finalists asked whether we have free will and whether we would owe anything to a conscious AI. Each finalist school gets £250.

When: 2027 round open now; entries due Monday 4 January 2027. Top five teams go to a final in Oxford (the 2026 final was on 24 March).

Uehiro PERC  uehiro.ox.ac.uk/perc

Th

The Ethics Cup (University of St Andrews; formerly the John Stuart Mill Cup)

30entryTeams of 14–18 year oldsvia school£90 per school (reduced rate £25 for schools on the organiser's eligibility list)

A team discusses a set of real ethical cases with another team. Marks go for careful reasoning and for responding to the other side, not for winning the argument. The case set is published in advance.

When: Regionals January 2027. Nearest: University of Warwick, 7 January 2027 (registration closes 23 October 2026, nine teams maximum); Dean Close School, Cheltenham, 20 January 2027 (six teams maximum). Final at St Andrews in the spring (2026 final was 28 April).

Ethics Cup  ethicscup.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/registration

Ps

#PhilosophyMatters student prize (British Philosophical Association and Association of Philosophy Teachers)

31entryPupils aged 16+ studying A level Philosophy (or a club), UKenter yourself

Complete the sentence "Philosophy matters to me because…" in no more than 70 more words. Prizes of £75 and £50.

When: 2026 deadline was noon, 2 March 2026, with an online prize-giving on 25 March. Confirm 2027 dates on the BPA site in the new year.

BPA page  bpa.ac.uk/philosophymatters-2026-student-prizes

Ph

Philosophy Essay Prize (Trinity College, Cambridge)

32paperYear 12enter yourself

Up to 2,000 words on one of three questions (2025 included "Does everything have an explanation?"). £600 first prize, split between you and the school.

When: The page still shows the 2025 round (deadline 30 May 2025). Trinity says 2027 information will be posted in February 2027; confirm then whether the philosophy prize is running.

Trinity  trin.cam.ac.uk/undergraduate/essay-prizes/philosophy
