Classical Civilisation beyond the classroom36 things to read, hear, watch and enter. Free unless the entry says otherwise.

Classics and Ancient History courses expect you to have read beyond the set texts and to know something of the scholarship. The podcasts and lectures below are where that is; the essay prizes are where you use it. Visit a museum with a Greek or Roman gallery and write down one object. Each time you do something, write one line in your commentary: what you did, one thing you learned, one question it left you with. That is your personal statement material.

Talks and lectures · 13 things

Watch

Ds

Jill Dash: Everything you need to know to read Homer's "Odyssey"

125mTED-Ed

A 5 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: The talk dwells on the Cyclops. Using Odyssey 9, argue whether Odysseus' boast to Polyphemus shows heroic kleos or the lack of restraint that costs him his crew.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/jill_dash_everything_you_need_to_know_to_read_homer_s_odyssey

Rb

Mark Robinson: Why should you read Virgil's "Aeneid"?

135mTED-Ed

A 5 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: Why might Augustus have wanted the Aeneid published against Virgil's wishes? Find one passage that supports your answer (for example the Parade of Heroes or the Shield) and one that complicates it.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/mark_robinson_why_should_you_read_virgil_s_aeneid

Wn

Matthew Winkler: What makes a hero?

144mTED-Ed

A 4 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: Test Campbell's hero's journey against Odysseus or Aeneas. Where does the pattern break, and what does the break tell you about Homer's or Virgil's idea of a hero? This talk is there to be argued with.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/matthew_winkler_what_makes_a_hero

Gl

Iseult Gillespie: The tragedy of Cassandra, the princess who predicted the fall of Troy

154mTED-Ed

A 4 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: Compare this retelling with the fall of Troy in Aeneid 2. Why does Virgil give the story to a Trojan narrator, and what does that do to the Greeks?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/iseult_gillespie_the_tragedy_of_cassandra_the_princess_who_predicted_the_fall_of_troy

Mc

Neil MacGregor: 2600 years of history in one object

1619mtalk

A 19 min talk.

Think about: Does the Cyrus Cylinder support or undercut the Greek picture of Persian kingship in Herodotus or Aeschylus' Persians? Give one point each way.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/neil_macgregor_2600_years_of_history_in_one_object

Rn

Mark Robinson: Why is Herodotus called "The Father of History"?

175mTED-Ed

A 5 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: Herodotus often reports stories without vouching for them. Take one story you know from him and decide what it tells us about Greek views of non-Greeks rather than about the non-Greeks themselves.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/mark_robinson_why_is_herodotus_called_the_father_of_history

Es

Stephen Esposito: The Greek tragedy of Oedipus' daughter

185mTED-Ed

A 5 min talk (TED-Ed).

Think about: Antigone sets divine law against the city's law. How would an Athenian audience have judged her? Compare how the gods' authority works in Oedipus the King.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/stephen_esposito_the_greek_tragedy_of_oedipus_daughter

Ca

Classics and Ancient History Essay Competition (St John's College, Oxford)

25essayY12 in any UK school; no prior study of classical subjects neededenter yourself

Up to 2,000 words on one question from four strands: literature, history, art and archaeology, philosophy. £100 book token for each strand winner, and a separate prize for the best essay from someone new to Classics.

When: Deadline late February 2027 (2026 was 22 February, 4pm); subject exploration day in April (2026 was 22 April). Confirm on the St John's page.

St John's  sjc.ox.ac.uk/access-and-outreach/our-programmes-sjc/subject-exploration-days/classics-essay-competition-2026

Aw

Ancient World and Classics Essay Competition (Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge)

26entryY12enter yourself

2,000–2,500 words on one of six questions, with a research log. 2026 questions included women in ancient Greece and judging ancient figures by modern standards. £200 first prize. No generative AI.

When: Questions usually published in October; deadline late February 2027 (2026 was 27 February, 6pm). Confirm on the Fitzwilliam page.

Fitzwilliam  fitz.cam.ac.uk/fitzwilliam-college-essay-competitions-2026

Mr

Mary Renault Classical Reception Essay Competition (St Hugh's College, Oxford)

27entryY12 and Y13enter yourself

2,000–4,000 words on how a classical text has been reworked in later literature, film or theatre. You choose the topic. 2026 winners wrote on Sartre's Aeschylus and modern musicals. No generative AI.

When: Deadline in July (2026 was 22 July, 2pm); winners announced in August. 2027 details not yet published: confirm.

St Hugh's  st-hughs.ox.ac.uk/prospective-students/outreach-at-st-hughs/essay-competitions/the-mary-renault-prize

Oc

Omnibus competitions: Gladstone Memorial Essay Prize and Sam Hood Translation Prize (Classical Association)

28essayUnder 19 and in full-time pre-university educationenter yourself

Essay: up to 2,000 words on one of eight topics (2027 includes gods in Homer and Virgil, and dramatic choruses); £200 first, £100 second. Translation: one passage of Latin or Greek (Livy, Virgil, Juvenal, Sophocles, Aristophanes, Lucian); £75 and a book. No AI and no tutors.

When: Deadline 2pm, Wednesday 7 July 2027. Full instructions appear in Omnibus 93.

CA page  classicalassociation.org/competition/omnibus-competitions
