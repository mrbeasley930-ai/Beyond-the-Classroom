Sixth form · Biology · 2026-27

Biology beyond the classroom34 things to watch, read, try and enter. Free unless the entry says otherwise.

Biology is the most over-subscribed science at university, and medicine, veterinary science and biomedical courses all ask what you have done beyond the syllabus. The Olympiad, the essay prizes and the placements below are the things interviewers ask about; the lectures and reading are how you get something to say. Each time you do something, write one line in your field notebook: what you did, one thing you learned, one question it left you with. That is your personal statement material.

Competitions · 5 things

Enter

Bb

British Biology Olympiad (UK Biology Competitions)

01paperY12 and Y13via school

Two 45-minute online multiple-choice papers sat in school in January, going well beyond A level. Top scorers go on to selection rounds at Warwick for the UK team.

When: 15–22 January 2027 (published). School registration opens 1 October 2026.

UKBC page  ukbiologycompetitions.org/british-biology-olympiad

Ib

Intermediate Biology Olympiad (UK Biology Competitions)

02entryY12 onlyvia school

The Y12 version of the Olympiad, sat in school in June. Good practice for the full Olympiad in Y13.

When: 4–18 June 2027 (published). Registration opens 17 April 2027.

UKBC page  ukbiologycompetitions.org/intermediate-biology-olympiad

Pk

Peterhouse Kelvin Sciences Essay Competition (Cambridge)

03essayY12 (penultimate year), UK and Irelandenter yourself

An essay of up to 2,000 words on one of six science questions. In 2026 the biology options included species conservation, chirality in biology and why lifespan varies between species.

When: 2026 deadline was 20 April, teacher verification by 4 May. Confirm 2027 questions and deadline on the Peterhouse site in the spring.

Peterhouse  pet.cam.ac.uk/peterhouse-kelvin-biological-sciences-essay-competition

Cb

Cambridge Biology Challenge (Homerton College)

04essayY10–Y13, UK schoolsenter yourself

A team competition: a new Cambridge-style biology question every three weeks, answered as a poster, video, podcast or essay. Selected teams are invited to Homerton in August.

When: Runs through the year; in 2025-26 the first question was released on 20 October 2025, with a new question every three weeks. Confirm 2026-27 start on the Homerton page.

Homerton  homerton.cam.ac.uk/cambridge-biology-challenge

Rs

Royal Society of Biology Young Photographer of the Year

05entry18 and under; RSB membership not neededenter yourselfNo fee listed

Photograph living things in their habitat to a set theme (2026: Colours of Nature). The winner receives £500 and an invitation to the Biology Week awards in London.

When: 2026 competition ran 10 March–5 May. Confirm 2027 theme and dates on the RSB site.

RSB page  rsb.org.uk/photo-competition.html

Talks and lectures · 14 things

Watch

Dd

Jennifer Doudna: How CRISPR lets us edit our DNA

0616mtalk

A 16 min talk.

Think about: Explain how Cas9 finds its target sequence using what you know of complementary base pairing (AQA 3.8.4). Which of her ethical concerns do you think is the most serious, and why?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/jennifer_doudna_how_crispr_lets_us_edit_our_dna

Mc

Maryn McKenna: What do we do when antibiotics don't work any more?

0717mtalk

A 17 min talk.

Think about: Use the AQA account of natural selection (3.4.4) to explain how antibiotic resistance spreads through a bacterial population. Which of her proposed measures would slow it most?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/maryn_mckenna_what_do_we_do_when_antibiotics_don_t_work_any_more

Bs

Bonnie Bassler: How bacteria "talk"

0818mtalk

A 18 min talk.

Think about: Quorum sensing switches genes on when a signal molecule reaches a threshold. Compare this with how oestrogen regulates transcription in AQA 3.8.2.2.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/bonnie_bassler_how_bacteria_talk

Kn

Rob Knight: How our microbes make us who we are

0917mtalk

A 17 min talk.

Think about: Pick one link he describes between gut microbes and a condition. How would you design a study to show the microbes cause it, rather than being correlated with it?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/rob_knight_how_our_microbes_make_us_who_we_are

Sm

Suzanne Simard: How trees talk to each other

1018mtalk

A 18 min talk.

Think about: Where do mycorrhizae appear in the AQA nutrient cycles (3.5.4)? Which of her claims about trees sharing carbon are supported by the evidence she shows, and which go further than it?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/suzanne_simard_how_trees_talk_to_each_other

Wl

Matt Walker: Sleep is your superpower

1119mtalk

A 19 min talk.

Think about: Choose two of his statistics. For each, say whether it shows correlation or causation and what data you would need before accepting it.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/matt_walker_sleep_is_your_superpower

Yn

Ed Yong: Zombie roaches and other parasite tales

1213mtalk

A 13 min talk.

Think about: Choose one parasite that changes its host's behaviour. Which parts of AQA 3.6 (receptors, nervous coordination, synapses) would the parasite have to act on?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/ed_yong_zombie_roaches_and_other_parasite_tales

Edmond Hui: How the heart actually pumps blood

134mTED-Ed

A 4 min talk (TED-Ed).

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/edmond_hui_how_the_heart_actually_pumps_blood
