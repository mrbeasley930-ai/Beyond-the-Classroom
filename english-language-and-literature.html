Sixth form · English Language and Literature · 2026-27

English Language and Literature beyond the classroom36 things to read, listen to, watch and enter. Free unless the entry says otherwise.

This course sits between linguistics and literature, and universities for either will ask what you have read and what you noticed about how it was written. Listen to the language podcasts for the linguistics; read the magazines and anthologies for the rest. Write down how a text works, not only what it says. Each time you do something, write one line in your reading journal: what you did, one thing you learned, one question it left you with. That is your personal statement material.

Talks and lectures · 13 things

Watch

Ly

Jamila Lyiscott: 3 ways to speak English

134mtalk

A 4 min talk.

Think about: Mark each point where she switches variety of English and what signals it (lexis, grammar, pronunciation). Compare with how voice works in Jacob Sam-La Rose's Breaking Silence or Malika Booker's Pepper Seed.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/jamila_lyiscott_3_ways_to_speak_english

Mc

John McWhorter: Txtng is killing language. JK!!!

1414mtalk

A 14 min talk.

Think about: McWhorter calls texting 'fingered speech'. List three features of spontaneous speech in the Paxman–Dizzee Rascal interview in the anthology. Which would appear in a text message, and which would not?

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/john_mcwhorter_txtng_is_killing_language_jk

Cr

Anne Curzan: What makes a word "real"?

1517mtalk

A 17 min talk.

Think about: Who decides what counts as a word? Find one word in the Sophie Arthur blog or the Lucy Mangan review in the anthology that a 1990 dictionary would not have had, and explain how it came into use.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/anne_curzan_what_makes_a_word_real

Lera Boroditsky: How language shapes the way we think

1614mtalk

A 14 min talk.

Watch on TED  ted.com/talks/lera_boroditsky_how_language_shapes_the_way_we_think

Competitions · 5 things

Enter

Ul

UK Linguistics Olympiad (UKLO), Advanced level

26paperYears 12–13 (Advanced); younger pupils may enter a higher levelvia school

Puzzles built from real languages you have never seen. No knowledge of linguistics is needed; you work out the patterns from the data. Try a past paper first.

When: Advanced Round 1 is sat in school in the second week of February; gold medallists go on to Round 2 in March (3.5 hours, exam conditions). 2027 dates are on the UKLO noticeboard; confirm.

UKLO teachers  uklo.org/teachers-room

Gp

Gould Prize for Essays in English Literature (Trinity College, Cambridge)

27entryY12enter yourself

1,500–2,500 words on one of Trinity's questions. £600 first prize, split between you and the school. No help from school or AI.

When: Trinity posts 2027 information in February 2027. The 2026 deadline was 12 noon, 31 July; results early September. Confirm.

Trinity  trin.cam.ac.uk/undergraduate/essay-prizes/english-literature

Tc

Thomas Campion English Prize (Peterhouse, Cambridge)

28essayY12enter yourself

An English essay on a set question. First prize £500; winners and commended entrants are invited to Peterhouse.

When: Questions in the spring; deadline usually March or April, with a teacher verification step a few days later. Confirm on the Peterhouse site.

Peterhouse  pet.cam.ac.uk/essay-prizes

Oy

Orwell Youth Prize 2027: 'Acts of Courage'

29essayYears 7–13; Y12–13 in the senior categoryenter yourself

Up to 1,500 words in any form: essay, journalism, short story, poetry. Send a draft by 3 March and you get written feedback before the final deadline.

When: Open now. Draft for feedback by 3 March 2027; final entries by 29 April 2027.

Orwell Youth Prize  orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-youth-prize/about/enter-the-orwell-youth-prize

Fy

Foyle Young Poets of the Year Award (Poetry Society)

30paperAged 11–17 on 31 July (most Y12; few Y13)enter yourself

Send up to three original poems. 100 winners each year, 15 of them top winners. Poems written with AI are not allowed.

When: 2026 deadline was midnight, 31 July 2026; 2027 round expected to follow the same pattern. Confirm.

Foyle rules  foyleyoungpoets.org/rules
